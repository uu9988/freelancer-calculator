Freelance Rate Calculator

A freelance rate calculator helps you estimate the hourly price you need to charge so your freelance business stays profitable, sustainable, and aligned with your income goals.

Why freelancers need a rate calculator

Many freelancers underprice their work because they focus only on client demand and not on the full cost of running a business. A simple calculator helps you translate your target income into a practical rate that covers expenses, taxes, and time spent on non-billable work.

How the calculator works

The calculator combines your desired take-home income, business expenses, estimated tax rate, and billable hours to show the minimum rate you need. It also helps you compare a conservative baseline rate with a more comfortable recommended rate for long-term growth.

What to enter

  • Target annual income: The amount you want to keep after taxes.
  • Working days and hours: Your realistic availability each week.
  • Vacation and holidays: Time off that reduces your billable capacity.
  • Business expenses: Software, tools, insurance, and other recurring costs.
  • Tax rate and billable time: Your expected tax burden and the share of your time that can be billed.

FAQ

What is a freelance rate calculator?

It is a planning tool that converts your desired income and business costs into a rate that better reflects the real value of your work.

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

You can calculate it by dividing your required revenue by your annual billable hours, then adjusting for taxes, expenses, and your preferred profit margin.

Why should freelancers use a rate calculator?

It helps protect your income, reduces pricing guesswork, and makes it easier to set rates that support both short-term cash flow and long-term sustainability.

How freelance rates are compared

Calculate a sustainable hourly baseline first, then use project scope and risk to decide whether hourly, daily, or fixed project pricing is the better fit.

Rate comparison formula

Hourly baseline = required revenue / billable hours. A project baseline can be estimated by multiplying that rate by delivery hours and adding direct costs and contingency.

Rate comparison example

A $70 hourly baseline applied to a 20-hour project gives $1,400 before direct costs. A fixed quote can then account for revision and schedule risk.
