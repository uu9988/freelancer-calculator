How the calculation works

The calculator first estimates the annual revenue needed to cover your take-home target, business expenses, and simplified tax assumption. It then divides that revenue by realistic billable hours rather than all working hours.

Current formulas

Required pre-tax revenue
(target take-home income + business expenses) ÷ (1 - estimated tax rate)

Minimum hourly rate
required pre-tax revenue ÷ annual billable hours

Recommended hourly rate
minimum hourly rate × 1.15

The 15% is the tool's current contingency buffer for planning gaps between projects, scope changes, and payment risk. It is not a guarantee that every quote should use the same final price.

Terms used in the results

Working hours
All time spent running the business, including client delivery, administration, sales, communication, bookkeeping, and learning.
Billable hours
The portion of working hours that can be invoiced to clients.
Required pre-tax revenue
The total client revenue needed before the calculator's estimated tax and business expenses are deducted.
Take-home income
The annual amount you want to keep after the model's estimated tax and business expenses.
Business expenses
Costs required to run the business, excluding personal living expenses.

How to use your result

  1. Treat the minimum rate as a planning floor, not necessarily your final client quote.
  2. Use the recommended rate as a starting point for pricing or negotiation.
  3. Adjust for project complexity, delivery risk, revision limits, urgency, and client value.
  4. If the result is much higher than your current rate, first review unrecorded expenses, non-billable work, and your billable percentage.
  5. Calculate again when your income goal, tax assumption, expenses, or working schedule changes.
  6. For fixed-price work, also account for project scope, direct costs, and revision risk.

Hourly rate calculator FAQ

What counts as billable time?

Billable time is work you can invoice to a client, such as agreed delivery work, client meetings, or project-specific research. Administration, marketing, bookkeeping, and general learning are working time but are usually not billable.

How do I estimate my billable percentage?

Review a typical week or recent time records and divide client-billable hours by total working hours. If you work 40 hours and can invoice 24 hours, enter 60%.

Which tax rate should I enter?

Enter a simplified estimated rate that is useful for planning in your situation. The calculator does not model tax brackets, deductions, VAT, sales tax, or local law, and it is not tax advice.

Does this calculator handle tax deductions?

No. The calculator treats business expenses and the tax rate as separate planning inputs. It does not calculate deductions, tax brackets, VAT, sales tax, or jurisdiction-specific rules, so consult a qualified adviser when needed.

Is the recommended rate the exact amount I should quote?

No. The calculator adds a 15% contingency buffer to the minimum rate. Use the recommended rate as a starting point, then consider project complexity, risk, revisions, and client value.

What if the calculated rate is higher than clients will pay?

First check whether your expenses, non-billable work, income target, and billable percentage are realistic. If the assumptions are sound, consider improving scope, positioning, efficiency, or project pricing instead of automatically charging below your minimum.

Should I use hourly or project pricing?

Use hourly pricing when scope or time is uncertain and tracking hours is practical. Use project pricing when scope and deliverables are clear, but include estimated hours, direct costs, revision risk, and contingency in the quote.
