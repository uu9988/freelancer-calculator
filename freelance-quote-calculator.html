Final client quote

Effective quoted hourly value
The final quote divided by estimated hours. It includes expenses, contingency, and discount effects, so it is not a pure hourly rate.

How the quote calculation works

This calculator builds a client-facing project quote. It does not estimate internal profit, generate an invoice, schedule payments, or calculate taxes.

Calculation formulas

Labor subtotal
estimated project hours × hourly rate

Base project amount
labor subtotal + direct project expenses

Contingency amount
base project amount × contingency buffer percentage

Quote before discount
base project amount + contingency amount

Discount amount
quote before discount × client discount percentage

Final client quote
quote before discount - discount amount

Effective quoted hourly value
final client quote ÷ estimated project hours

No automatic profit margin or hidden formula is added. Contingency is an explicit project-risk buffer, and direct expenses remain project costs rather than personal income.

How to use your quote

  1. Confirm the scope and deliverables before sending the quote.
  2. State how many revision rounds are included.
  3. Keep a reasonable contingency when the scope contains genuine uncertainty.
  4. Offer a discount only for a clear business reason, not automatically.
  5. List project-specific expenses separately so the client understands what they cover.
  6. Include a payment schedule and an expiry date in the written quote.
  7. Use a change order or revised quote when the project scope changes.
  8. Do not ignore your minimum acceptable rate merely to match the client's budget.

Freelance quote calculator FAQ

What should I include in estimated project hours?

Include research, meetings, production, revisions, testing, delivery, and reasonable project administration. Use a realistic estimate based on the agreed scope rather than only the visible production time.

What counts as a direct project expense?

Include costs that exist because of this project, such as stock assets, travel, subcontractors, printing, special software licenses, or materials. Keep general personal spending separate.

How much contingency should I add?

Choose a percentage that reflects reasonable uncertainty, coordination, and minor changes in this specific scope. Contingency is a planning buffer, not a hidden charge or automatic profit margin.

Should I give every client a discount?

No. Use a discount only when there is a clear business reason, such as reduced scope, a longer commitment, or another documented trade-off. A discount should not silently reduce an already unsustainable rate.

Is the final quote the same as an invoice?

No. The final quote is a planning amount to present before work begins. An invoice requests payment according to the accepted scope, contract, milestones, and payment terms.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. It does not calculate VAT, sales tax, income tax, deductions, or location-specific legal requirements. Confirm any required tax treatment with a qualified local professional; this tool is not tax or legal advice.

What if the client changes the project scope?

Document the change, estimate its effect on hours and expenses, and use a change order or revised quote before completing the additional work. Do not rely on a small contingency to absorb a materially different scope.

Should I quote hourly or use a fixed project price?

Hourly pricing can suit uncertain or evolving work, while a fixed project price can suit a clearly defined scope. This calculator uses hours and a rate to build a client-facing project amount, but the commercial format remains your decision.
