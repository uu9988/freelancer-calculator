Freelance Income Calculator

Estimate annual and monthly freelance revenue from your average hourly rate and realistic billable capacity, then account for business expenses and a simplified tax reserve.

Use one currency for every monetary input. The calculator does not convert currencies, and the estimated tax rate is a planning assumption rather than tax advice.

Income assumptions

Enter your own rate, billable workload, annual costs, and estimated effective tax rate. Results appear only after you submit the form.

Use the same currency for the hourly rate and expenses. Monetary results follow that same assumption; no currency conversion is performed.

Your income estimate

Enter your assumptions and select Calculate Income.

Gross revenue
The total amount clients pay before expenses and taxes.
Business expenses
Operating costs required to run the freelance business.
Tax reserve
A simplified planning reserve, not a final tax calculation.
Planning income
Gross revenue minus business expenses and the simplified tax reserve. It is an estimate, not guaranteed take-home income.

How the income calculation works

The calculator starts with the revenue your billable work could produce. It then subtracts business expenses and a simplified tax reserve to estimate planning income.

Calculation formulas

Annual gross revenue
average hourly rate × billable hours per week × working weeks per year

Average monthly gross revenue
annual gross revenue ÷ 12

Estimated taxable planning base
max(annual gross revenue - annual business expenses, 0)

Estimated tax reserve
estimated taxable planning base × estimated effective tax rate

Annual planning income
annual gross revenue - annual business expenses - estimated tax reserve

Average monthly planning income
annual planning income ÷ 12

Effective planning income percentage
annual planning income ÷ annual gross revenue × 100

When expenses exceed gross revenue, the taxable planning base and tax reserve are zero. Planning income may be negative, which represents an estimated loss.

How to use your income estimate

  1. Keep the estimated tax reserve separate from money available for business or personal spending.
  2. Update business expenses regularly using actual subscriptions, equipment, insurance, and professional costs.
  3. Use real time records to adjust billable hours per week instead of assuming every working hour can be invoiced.
  4. If planning income is below your target, compare raising your rate, increasing realistic billable hours, and reducing expenses.
  5. Do not treat gross revenue as personal disposable income.
  6. Recalculate when tax rules, rates, expenses, or workload change.
  7. Consult a qualified local professional when you need an accurate tax calculation.

Freelance income calculator FAQ

What is the difference between gross revenue and planning income?

Gross revenue is the total amount clients pay before expenses and taxes. Planning income subtracts annual business expenses and the calculator's simplified tax reserve from gross revenue.

What counts as a billable hour?

A billable hour is time you can invoice to a client for agreed work. Administration, sales, general communication, bookkeeping, and learning are working time but usually are not billable.

Which tax rate should I enter?

Enter a reasonable estimated effective rate for your location, income, and business situation. It is a simplified planning assumption, not a prediction of your final tax bill.

Should I include personal living expenses?

No. Enter costs required to operate your freelance business, such as software, equipment, insurance, accounting, and marketing. Keep personal housing, food, and other living costs separate.

What if my expenses are higher than my revenue?

The taxable planning base and tax reserve become zero, while planning income becomes negative. The calculator displays an estimated loss so you can review pricing, billable capacity, or costs.

Does this calculator include tax deductions?

No. It does not model tax brackets, deductions, VAT, sales tax, or jurisdiction-specific law. The tax reserve is a simplified planning estimate and is not tax advice.

Can I use monthly income instead of an hourly rate?

This version starts with an hourly rate and billable capacity. If you plan from monthly income, convert the target into an hourly assumption or use the monthly gross result to compare scenarios.
