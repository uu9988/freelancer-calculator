How the expense calculation works

The calculator separates costs that normally recur every month from annual or occasional purchases. It then compares the combined annual total with expected revenue and billable capacity. It excludes personal living expenses, income tax, and project-specific subcontractor costs.

How to use your expense estimate

  1. Separate monthly recurring costs from annual or occasional purchases.
  2. Use actual bills, bank records, and payment records wherever possible.
  3. Include small subscriptions that continue every month, because they add up over a year.
  4. Record planned equipment and professional development as annual expenses.
  5. Keep personal living expenses separate from business operating costs.
  6. Compare total annual expenses with expected annual revenue.
  7. Use the cost per billable hour to check whether your hourly rate can cover business overhead.
  8. Recalculate regularly as actual spending, revenue, or billable capacity changes.

Freelance expense calculator FAQ

What counts as a freelance business expense?

A freelance business expense is a cost incurred to operate the business, such as software, workspace, insurance, marketing, equipment, education, or business travel. Whether a cost is deductible depends on local rules and your circumstances.

What is the difference between monthly and annual expenses?

Monthly expenses usually recur throughout the year, such as subscriptions or workspace costs. Annual expenses happen less often or vary by year, such as equipment purchases, training, and business travel.

Should equipment purchases be included?

Include equipment you expect to buy, repair, or replace for the business during the year. This calculator treats the amount as an annual operating estimate and does not determine accounting or tax treatment.

Should personal expenses be included?

No. Keep personal housing, food, transportation, and other living expenses separate unless you have identified a legitimate business portion under the rules that apply to you.

What is an operating expense ratio?

The operating expense ratio is total annual business expenses divided by expected annual revenue. It estimates what percentage of revenue is needed to cover the operating costs entered here.

How do expenses affect my hourly rate?

Business expenses must be covered by client revenue. The expense cost per billable hour shows how much of each invoiced hour is needed for the annual operating costs entered in the calculator.

Does this calculator determine tax deductions?

No. It does not determine deductibility, depreciation, tax categories, or jurisdiction-specific treatment. It is a planning tool and does not provide tax, legal, or accounting advice.

How often should I review my business expenses?

Review expenses regularly and whenever subscriptions, workspace costs, equipment plans, revenue expectations, or billable capacity change. Comparing estimates with actual records keeps the plan useful.
