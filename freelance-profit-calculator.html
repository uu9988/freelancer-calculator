Freelance Profit Calculator

Estimate how much profit a freelance project may produce after internal labor, direct expenses, allocated overhead, and payment processing fees.

Use one currency for every monetary input. The calculator performs no currency conversion. Internal hourly cost is not necessarily your client rate, and this tool does not calculate income tax. Example figures are not recommended defaults.

Enter the expected client revenue and the complete internal costs of delivering this project. Use 0 for expense or percentage fields that do not apply.

Your project profit estimate

Enter your project assumptions and select Calculate Profit.

Internal labor cost
The internal cost assigned to the time required to deliver the project.
Direct project expenses
Subcontractor payments and other costs that belong specifically to this project.
Overhead allocation
A share of ongoing business operating costs assigned to the project.
Payment processing fee
An estimated deduction made by a payment processor or platform.
Total project cost
The full internal cost estimate included by this calculator.
Project profit and margin
Profit is client revenue minus total project cost. Margin is the percentage of revenue retained as project profit. Neither amount includes income tax.

How the profit calculation works

This calculator compares project revenue with the internal cost of performing the work. It does not create a client quote, estimate long-term income, generate an invoice, or calculate income tax.

Calculation formulas

Internal labor cost
internal project hours × internal hourly cost

Direct project expenses
subcontractor costs + other direct project expenses

Base project cost
internal labor cost + direct project expenses

Overhead allocation amount
base project cost × overhead allocation percentage

Payment processing fee amount
client revenue × payment processing fee percentage

Total project cost
base project cost + overhead allocation amount + payment processing fee amount

Project profit
client revenue - total project cost

Profit margin
project profit ÷ client revenue × 100

Cost percentage
total project cost ÷ client revenue × 100

Revenue per internal hour
client revenue ÷ internal project hours

Profit per internal hour
project profit ÷ internal project hours

Negative profit and margin remain visible when estimated cost exceeds revenue. No hidden markup, client discount, or automatic quote logic is added.

How to use your profit estimate

  1. Compare client revenue with the complete project cost before accepting the work.
  2. Include meetings, communication, revisions, testing, administration, and delivery time.
  3. Track subcontractor payments and project-specific expenses separately.
  4. Adjust overhead allocation using actual business operating data.
  5. Account for fees that a payment platform may deduct.
  6. When margin is too low, review the scope, client price, time estimate, and costs.
  7. Recalculate whenever the project scope changes.
  8. After delivery, compare actual profit with the original estimate.
  9. Do not treat project profit as personal after-tax income.

Freelance profit calculator FAQ

What is project profit?

Project profit is client revenue minus the internal labor cost, direct project expenses, allocated overhead, and estimated payment processing fee for that project.

What is a good freelance project profit margin?

There is no single margin that is appropriate for every freelancer or project. A useful target depends on service type, risk, demand, operating model, workload, and business goals. Compare your estimate with your own historical results.

What is the difference between profit and revenue?

Revenue is the amount expected from the client before project costs are deducted. Profit is what remains after the project costs included by this calculator are subtracted.

Should my working time count as a project cost?

Yes. Assigning an internal hourly cost to your production, research, meetings, revisions, testing, administration, and delivery time prevents unpaid effort from disappearing from the profitability estimate.

What expenses should be included in project profit?

Include subcontractors, project-specific software or assets, licenses, travel, materials, hosting, printing, equipment rental, allocated overhead, and expected payment processing fees. Avoid counting the same expense twice.

Does this calculator include income tax?

No. It does not calculate income tax, tax brackets, deductions, VAT, sales tax, or location-specific requirements. It is a project planning tool and not legal, accounting, or tax advice.

What does a negative profit margin mean?

A negative margin means the estimated project costs are greater than client revenue. Review the scope, time estimate, internal cost, direct expenses, overhead, payment fees, and client price before accepting the work.

How can I improve project profitability?

Improve the accuracy of time estimates, control avoidable costs, clarify scope and revisions, reduce unpaid work, and set a client price that reflects the project's full delivery cost and value.
