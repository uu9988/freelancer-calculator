Freelance Invoice Calculator

Estimate how much should appear on a freelance invoice and how much the client still owes after discounts, estimated sales tax or VAT, and payments already received.

Use one currency for every monetary input. The calculator performs no currency conversion. Tax rules depend on location and business circumstances, and the percentage entered here is only an estimate. This tool is not legal, tax, or accounting advice and does not generate an official invoice.

Invoice assumptions

Enter the completed work, agreed reimbursable expenses, discount, estimated tax, and payments already received. Use 0 for fields that do not apply.

All monetary inputs must use the same currency. No payment fee, late fee, tip, income tax, hidden markup, or automatic profit margin is added.

Your invoice estimate

Enter the completed work and payment details, then select Calculate Invoice.

Invoice total
The subtotal after discount plus the estimated sales tax or VAT entered above.
Balance due
The invoice total minus deposits, retainers, milestone payments, or previous payments already received.
Client credit or overpayment
The absolute amount by which recorded payments exceed the calculated invoice total.

How the invoice calculation works

This calculator estimates an amount to place on an invoice after work is completed or reaches an agreed billing milestone. It is separate from the Quote Calculator, which helps set a proposed price before work begins.

Calculation formulas

Hourly labor amount
billable hours × hourly rate

Subtotal before discount
hourly labor amount + fixed-fee services + reimbursable expenses

Discount amount
subtotal before discount × client discount percentage

Subtotal after discount
subtotal before discount - discount amount

Estimated sales tax or VAT
subtotal after discount × estimated tax percentage

Invoice total
subtotal after discount + estimated sales tax or VAT

Remaining amount
invoice total - amount already paid

A positive remaining amount is shown as a balance due. A zero amount is shown as paid in full. If payments exceed the invoice total, the calculator shows the absolute difference as a client credit or overpayment.

How to use your invoice estimate

  1. Enter hours for work that has actually been completed or reached an agreed billing milestone.
  2. Keep fixed-fee services separate from services billed by the hour.
  3. Add only project expenses the client has agreed to reimburse.
  4. Confirm any client discount before issuing the invoice.
  5. Determine whether sales tax or VAT applies under the rules relevant to the transaction.
  6. Subtract deposits, retainers, milestone payments, or other payments already received.
  7. Check the contract, completed scope, payment terms, and payment record before sending the invoice.
  8. Keep the actual invoice and supporting payment records in your normal business system.

This estimate does not replace an accounting system or determine the legal wording, numbering, dates, tax identifiers, or recordkeeping rules required for an official invoice.

Freelance invoice calculator FAQ

What should be included on a freelance invoice?

A complete invoice normally identifies the freelancer and client, lists the completed services, shows quantities or hours and rates, includes approved expenses and applicable tax, states the total and amount already paid, and gives the due date and payment terms. Confirm local requirements for your business.

What is the difference between a quote and an invoice?

A quote presents a proposed price before work begins. An invoice requests payment for completed work or an agreed billing milestone under the accepted scope and payment terms.

Should reimbursable expenses be added to an invoice?

Add expenses only when the client agreed that they are reimbursable. Keep receipts or other records and describe the charges clearly on the actual invoice.

Should a client discount be applied before tax?

This calculator applies the discount before estimated sales tax or VAT. The legally required tax treatment of discounts varies by location and transaction, so confirm the applicable rule before issuing an invoice.

Does this calculator determine whether I must charge sales tax or VAT?

No. It applies only the simplified percentage you enter and does not determine registration, exemptions, tax location, invoice wording, or legal obligations. It is not tax, legal, or accounting advice.

How should I record a client deposit?

Enter a deposit, retainer, milestone payment, or previous payment as the amount already paid. Keep the actual payment record and show the credit clearly on the formal invoice.

What happens if the client has already paid more than the invoice total?

The calculator shows the difference as a client credit or overpayment rather than a negative balance due. Review the payment record and decide how the credit should be handled.

Does this tool generate an official invoice?

No. It only estimates invoice amounts and the remaining balance. It does not create a numbered invoice, PDF, legal document, accounting record, payment request, or tax filing.
